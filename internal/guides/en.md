# English writing

Lead with the useful answer. Use concrete subjects, active verbs and familiar words. Fit the audience and genre. Support factual claims, preserve uncertainty and distinguish evidence from interpretation. Remove canned transitions, empty praise and repeated conclusions.

Apply antislop during drafting and revision without a separate request. Remove generic openings, inflated claims, forced contrasts and paragraphs that repeat rather than develop the point. Prefer concrete actors and actions to vague abstractions. Vary structure where the meaning benefits; do not replace every paragraph with a list or shorten useful explanation for its own sake.

Preserve facts, quotations, numbers, identifiers, conditions, uncertainty, deliberate voice and requested formatting. Do not invent evidence or polish away risks. Change wording only for a specific reader-facing problem; an isolated word or punctuation choice does not establish poor writing.

Revise while writing. No separate copy editor or chief editor is required by default.
